> Project Overview <

According to the FTC Game Manual, the Engineering Portfolio is a maximum 15-page document (plus one (1) cover page) that tells the story of the team, their robot's design and evolution throughout the season, and their outreach impact. To me, this was a chance to show how my team, the Tech Heroes, approached the challenges of the season and the growth we experienced as a result. It also put everything I learned and contributed toward the team to the test as this was my final year of participation in the program.

> The Problem <

In previous years, we struggled to effectively document our team's progress and achivements throughout the season. We often started working on the portfolio once the new year had begun, which was far too late to capture the full scope of our work.

> The Approach & Design Direction <

Our approach was to use a centralized logging system via Discord forums and threads to keep track of our progress and achievements throughout the season. This allowed us to easily reference and curate relevant information in the portfolio.

> Outcome <

I was very proud of how this year went! Although our robot malfunctioned during the competition netting us nearly last place in qualification matches during that season's Philadelphia Qualifier event, the documentation of our journey allowed us to win the Think Award. Per the FTC Game Manual, the Think Award is given to the team that best reflects the engineering design process through clear, detailed documentation in their Engineering Portfolio.
